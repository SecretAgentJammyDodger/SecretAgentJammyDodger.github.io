Quaternions, Euler Angles, Versors, Topology part 1

First documented 25th August 2026

Trying to understand mathematics beyond things like calculus and 3 dimensional vectors has always meant
trying to figure out and reverse engineer just what on earth all the crazy names, symbols, theories, and proofs mean.
As someone that is currently doing video game programming in Unity, occasionally problems have come up
wherein the so-called "Euler Angles" prove insufficient for rotations and/or calculations, and instead
a so-called "Quaternion" solution is chosen.

Those of you who are reading this who are like-minded might be somewhat triggered by the use of "so-called"
in such a manner, as despite popular misconception, it does not mean "this is what it is called", but rather
puts into focus the dubious, suspicious, or insufficient naming of the thing that we are talking of.

That is all to say, that Euler Angles may also be called, perhaps more properly, Tait-Bryan Angles,
and more surprisingly and importantly to some, that the Quaternions that we typically refer to
in the common engines such as Unity or Unreal, are more properly named Versors -
Unit Quaternions, and those with "norm" or magnitude 1, are properly referred to as Versors.
This distinction is indeed quite important, more important than one might initially think or feel.
But more about that later.

All of this was kind of spurred off by a random math book that I read, that stated something about
how the real reason the these Euler or Tait-Bryan Angles are insufficient for properly representing
rotations in 3D space is because rotations, or the paths traced by a rotation, somehow conforms onto
the surface of a torus, whereas the Unit Quaternions or Versors instead has rotations or paths traced
by them happening on the surface of a sphere.

This was a fascinating concept that seemed to finally make some sense to me as to why Euler Angles didn't
always work but (Unit) Quaternions do.
Of course, after some more digging, I learned that in fact the Versors are indeed not themselves entirely sufficient
and that something else is needed too...
This is where I come to the meat of the discussion and digging that I did yesterday and this morning.
So, allegedly, or according to the theories of topology (not strictly polygonal, as is the common meaning
of the term as used in game dev mostly by artists) of broader mathematics, there are these things called Topological Spaces.

So, the general assertions of the theory that I believe are important are that:

  • Versors live in a topological space known as S^3 or a 3-sphere.
  • Quaternions live in a topological space known as R^4, and the 3-sphere exists inside R^4.
  • The algebra of Quaternions takes place in R^4
  • The algebra of Versors takes place in a space known as SU(2), which lets the Versors have single solutions?
  • The 3D vectors that we are used to and are relatively straightforward exist inside R^3.
  • A rotation of an Euler Angle creates a space known as S^1 or a 1-sphere; a circle.
  • The 3 rotations of Euler Angles when put together make a 3-torus: "S^1 x S^1 x S^1 = T^3"
  • (don't ask me why the previous statement, I haven't gotten there yet)
  • S^1 is an algebraic space or structure that exists inside C, the space of complex numbers.

So yeah, there's a good chance I have butchered some of the terminology, or am misunderstanding some things,
but the basic gist is too much to write at this time so I will come back to this later...
